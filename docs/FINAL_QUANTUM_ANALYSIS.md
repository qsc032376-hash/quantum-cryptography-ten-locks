**Scope and release identity.** This document reviews all ten Team 3 solutions and `SCORING.md` at the verified frozen v5 source, [`41c7c6775b8fc499c2acdd878b4829bd17e1210d`](https://github.com/qsc032376-hash/quantum-cryptography-ten-locks/tree/41c7c6775b8fc499c2acdd878b4829bd17e1210d). As of this review, no later frozen v6 release ref was found; Junrui's exact final package and its metrics remain unconfirmed. ## What is scored

`SCORING.md` awards 40 points for solved tiers, 15 for simplicity, 25 for presentation and 20 for analysis. Full tier credit requires both a matching plaintext hash and an executed circuit. The hash checks the answer, not whether measurements actually produced the key. For each executed circuit, the Checker transpiles to the official basis and scores the one with the most two-qubit gates, breaking ties by depth; `build_circuit()` alone is not scored. Simplicity weights are 40% two-qubit gates, 30% depth, 20% total gates and 10% active qubits, using best-eligible-team / team ratios. Actual points depend on other eligible teams. Measurements, resets and barriers are excluded from gate counts. Shots, oracle calls, compilation and classical preprocessing matter to analysis even when not directly in that formula. The official solve and scoring-transpile limits are each 120 seconds.

## Ten tiers: measurement to cryptographic result

| Tier | Actual v5 approach | Mathematical mechanism and recovery |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Bernstein–Vazirani; one 24-input query, target in \(|-\rangle\) | For \(f_s(x)=s\cdot x\pmod2\), the oracle kicks back \((-1)^{s\cdot x}\). Hadamards transform this character to \(|s\rangle\); one ideal measurement gives the key. |
| 2 | BB84; each channel signal measured once, sifted by basis | Matching bases agree ideally; mismatches are random. Under random-basis intercept–resend, half the sifted bits were intercepted in the wrong basis and half of those are wrong, giving expected QBER 25%. A finite sample fluctuates. Public check bits compare links; remaining matched measurements go to reconciliation. This is an attack model, not a general BB84 security proof. |
| 3 | Deutsch–Jozsa; one query for each of 24 promised six-input oracles | After phase kickback, all-zero amplitude is \(2^{-n}\sum_x(-1)^{f(x)}\). Its magnitude is 1 for a constant function and 0 for a balanced function. This exact classification requires the promise and ideal operations. |
| 4 | Simon; 10 input plus 10 output qubits, repeated samples | Under the two-to-one promise \(f(x)=f(x\oplus s)\), interference produces \(y\cdot s=0\) over \(\mathbb F_2\). Collect independent equations and solve the nullspace by Gaussian elimination. Finite samples may lack sufficient rank. |
| 5 | Grover; 10 search bits, two-byte known-prefix predicate, 25 iterations | Oracle marks matching keys and diffusion reflects around the uniform state. Only measured candidates are checked against the public prefix before decryption. Work and phase flag must be uncomputed. |
| 6 | Grover plus a measured calibration flag | Search marks the first-byte predicate. A pilot estimates the marked population and chooses a bounded iteration count; measured candidates are filtered classically using the challenge checks. The one-byte predicate may admit multiple keys. |
| 7 | QPE; 14 counting bits and supplied one-qubit eigenstate/unitary, with an interference check | If \(U|u\rangle=e^{2\pi i\phi}|u\rangle\), controlled \(U^{2^j}\) encodes \(\phi\); the full inverse QFT estimates it. The implementation retains the controlled unitary's complete phase and maps counting bit \(j\) to classical bit \(j\). |
| 8 | Shor order finding for public \(N=35\); 12 counting and six work bits | Measurements give phase estimates related to \(s/r\), where \(r=\operatorname{ord}_N(a)\). Continued fractions and public modular checks yield order candidates, then gcds recover factors when conditions below hold. |
| 9 | Same order-finding structure for \(N=77\); v5 has 14 counting and seven work bits | Controlled public modular powers, full inverse QFT, measurement and validated order recovery feed the classical RSA adapter. The v2 research candidate separately adds one clean synthesis wire. |
| 10 | Discrete logarithm with two six-bit Fourier registers and seven work bits | For \(A=g^a\) of known order \(r\), the reversible map \(f(x,z)=g^xA^z\) is constant on shifts satisfying \(\Delta x+a\Delta z=0\pmod r\). Fourier frequencies ideally obey \(v\equiv au\pmod r\). Finite-grid rounding can produce inconsistent or partial equations; v5 only verifies measurement-derived full-order candidates against \(g^a=A\), then derives the Diffie–Hellman shared value classically. |

For Grover, with \(N\) search items and \(M\) marked, \(\sin^2\theta=M/N\), and after \(k\) ideal iterations success is \(\sin^2((2k+1)\theta)\). The usual \(O(\sqrt{N/M})\) query bound assumes a suitable oracle and known or estimated marked fraction. Oracle construction, verification and repeated shots are extra work.

For Shor, a useful even order \(r\) with \(a^{r/2}\not\equiv-1\pmod N\) yields nontrivial factors through \(\gcd(a^{r/2}\pm1,N)\) for the challenge's two-prime setting. Not every measurement or choice of \(a\) gives useful information; validation and bounded retries matter. The work-register state need not start as a single eigenstate: \(|1\rangle\) decomposes into eigenstates of modular multiplication, and QPE samples corresponding phases. Padding states outside \([0,N)\) are fixed to make a complete reversible permutation.

## Actual circuit example: Tier 1

The following is the actual logical layout of `submissions/team3/tier01/solution.py`, compacted by grouping its 24 identical input wires. The oracle is the challenge-provided reversible \(U_f:|x,y\rangle\mapsto|x,y\oplus f_s(x)\rangle\); its internal gates are not invented here. The target is not measured.

```text
24 inputs |0> -- H⊗24 --[ U_f ]-- H⊗24 -- measure q[j] → c[j]
                           |
  target  |0> -- X -- H --[ U_f ]------------------------------
```

Since \(XH\) as a time-ordered preparation means X then H, the target is \(|-\rangle\); the result is \(|s\rangle| -\rangle\) in the ideal model. The displayed gate ordering is temporal, not the conventional right-to-left matrix product.

For the Tier 8 controlled block, a complete complex-operator audit covered each actual control/work/dirty input and inverse, including phases; for Tier 9, exact permutation arguments, full-domain block tests, clean-work restoration and finite sampled results support correctness. These tests are not a dense full-system unitary proof or a guarantee for all seeds. For Tier 10, the independent state comparison tests the prepared circuit state, while the component construction supplies a broader compositional argument; it is not an arbitrary-input full 19-qubit operator comparison. No full-system matrix is needed to claim more than the evidence supports.

Exact fanout illustrates the proof obligation: for targets sharing a predicate and pivot \(p\), let \(F\) fan out \(p\) to the other targets. \(F\,\mathrm{MCX}(C,p)\,F^{-1}\) flips every target exactly when the predicate holds; with control off, the fanouts cancel. Complete decompositions must preserve complex phases and restore clean or borrowed workspace. An uncorrected relative-phase Toffoli cannot simply replace an exact Toffoli.

## Scaling, simulation and hardware

- **Query complexity versus implementation:** BV and Deutsch–Jozsa use one quantum oracle query under their promises; exact deterministic classical Deutsch–Jozsa can require exponentially many queries. Simon obtains polynomially many samples versus exponential classical query complexity in its oracle model. Grover uses quadratically fewer oracle queries than unstructured classical search, not a universal exponential speedup. Scalable quantum order finding and discrete log use polynomial resources in the input bit length given efficient reversible arithmetic; small-instance demonstrations do not establish a practical cryptanalytic break.
- **Toy compilation cost:** The Tier 8–10 implementations synthesize public permutations from full small tables. Those tables grow exponentially in work-register width, so these particular compilers are not scalable modular arithmetic. Include classical preprocessing, transpilation, repeated shots, verification and error correction when assessing total cost.
- **Two-qubit count and depth:** Count measures the number of scored two-wire gates; depth measures sequential gate layers under the scoring model. Neither alone determines wall time, and separate per-tier depths should not be described as one circuit's depth. The simulator's Aer-native circuit may differ substantially from the scored basis circuit.
- **Classical simulation:** A generic \(n\)-qubit complex128 statevector uses \(16\cdot2^n\) bytes before overhead; an extra active qubit can double this ideal storage. Runtime also depends on gate representation, native operations, parallelism, compilation, memory and environment. Cardinal is a classical CPU cluster running Aer; its 10/10 results are finite simulator samples, not a quantum-device experiment.
- **Real devices and noise:** A real device adds connectivity routing and SWAPs, calibration-dependent gate/readout errors, decoherence, crosstalk and finite shot noise. Reducing two-qubit gates or depth may reduce exposure but does not prove improved hardware fidelity or execution time. Longer coherent circuits, imperfect ancilla cleanup and phase errors can spoil interference. Assess with calibrated noise models or hardware trials, uncertainty and appropriate mitigation; none is demonstrated by OSC results. Fault-tolerant operation adds encoding, logical gates and error-correction overhead absent from these ideal toy counts.
